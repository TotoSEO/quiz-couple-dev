# What buyers say about interactive couple books (research notes, 7 November 2026)

Customer-voice research for a possible print-on-demand interactive couple book
(guided journal, fill-in book, bucket list with photo spaces, quiz/game book) by
quiz-couple.com.

## 0. Method, access, and how far to trust this

### Who buys

- **Mostly women, buying for a boyfriend or husband or for both of them.** Most
  reviewers who name a relationship are girlfriends or wives (Kaycee Lee,
  Jody, Nikita, Heather, Chloe Z., Kim S., Jade, vany2fr...). Men appear
  mostly as the people who receive the book (Cullen McAlpin, John Frias on
  Goodreads) or as the reluctant partner ("Hubby said no way am I reading
  this", below). There are a few male buyers (Jason Rice, Oliver Leggatt and
  Charles on Trustpilot; Aaron Johnson on Goodreads).
- **Newlyweds and engaged couples** are a core market for multi-year journals
  (Kim S.: "We are newlyweds, so I'm excited to see how our answers change
  over the years.").
- **Friends and parents buy for a couple.** Wedding gifts, adult children.
- **Gaps that buyers point out:** older couples (Jenna Scribbles on Kusi
  wants a version for "couples who've been together a long time or who are
  older - 60s, 70s."), parents of young children (Mario B., below), and
  people who are not cis, heterosexual and monogamous (Troy Zaher, below).

- "The only improvement would be just a few more lines; I'm wordy!!" (Kim S.,

- "What I don't like is how there are only a few lines for each person to

## 4. Usage patterns

- **Evening or bedtime ritual:** Kim S. ("right before bed each night"),
  Alexie (every evening before bed), Char Anderson (*A Year of Us*, in the
  evening).
- **Daily intent rarely holds:**
  - "I certainly don't see myself actually completing this book in one year."
    / "I would be pleased as punch if I actually remembered to do this every
    day for a week." (Erin Cataldi, *A Year of Us*)
  - "obviously we haven't been doing it one day at a time like it suggests."
    (Troy Zaher, who filled it over two years)
  - "Initially, we tried writing them completely separately (on different
    days), but then we would wind up never getting back to it." (Velena,
    https://www.glamorousgrowth.com/journal-prompts-for-couples/)
- **Together or apart:** the users who explain their method answer separately
  and then share. Velena: "Writing them down together (like collabing on
  answers) isn't really as beneficial either". Her method is to write on
  separate papers, then come together and talk *(paraphrase)*. Ramona Mead
  felt "put on the spot" answering together and suggests writing separately
  first.
- **Hybrid with the phone:** Heather texts the question of the day to her
  boyfriend. Kenny Bellew (Kusi): "My wife keeps this book on her phone". The
  Healthline writer used her phone's journal app while her boyfriend used a
  paper notebook *(paraphrase)*, https://www.healthline.com/health/i-tried-couples-journaling
- **Pass the book back and forth:** in a parent-and-child "back & forth"
  journal (4.7 from 119 Amazon ratings), users "add a page or two whenever we
  feel like it" "and then leave the book for the other person to take a turn."
  https://www.selfpublishingtitans.com/books/detail/2124 (not a couple book,
  but the same mechanic could work for a couple)
- **Reading it again:** reported for multi-year journals (BuzzFeed couples
  journal reviewer, Alexie, Kim S.'s hopes). Fill-in books are read again as
  keepsakes (Nikita).
- **Photo spaces:** Ryan & Alex did 21 of 50 dates. Each date ends with a
  photo and a few lines, and the photo box is small, so a phone print has to
  be the right size *(paraphrase)*, https://www.ryanandalex.com/adventure-challenge-book/ .
  TammyJo Eckhart: "The space for photos is limited." The Vol. 2 review
  summary notes "the built-in sticky strips for instant photos are separate,
  and there are much less dates"
  (https://ca.theadventurechallenge.com/fr/products/couples-edition-vol-2,
  an AI-generated summary on the page).
- **Social media:** the reveal of a filled-in book gets filmed and posted
  (TikTok topic pages and videos above). Fill-in books are published as
  "TikTok bestseller" titles. Givers search TikTok for answer ideas.

## 5. Data on completion, abandonment, repeat purchase

- **No hard data found** on completion or abandonment rates. Neither
  publishers nor researchers publish them, and Healthline notes there are
  limited studies on couples' journaling.
- SEO listicles state figures with no source, for example "couples who miss
  weeks often abandon the book entirely around month three" and "a 365-day
  commitment sounds romantic in the checkout cart and impossible by week
  three" (thehappylovedlife.com, thezealoflove.com). Do not quote them as
  facts. A search engine even attributed one of them to the wrong site
  (boostmatches.com, which does not contain it).
- **Repeat purchase does happen:**
  - a second copy of the same journal ("we ordered a second copy", BuzzFeed);
  - a second journal after finishing one (Alexie);
  - the sequel ("We love the first book so much that it has improved our
    relationship which leading to marriage.", William, Vol. 2);
  - several titles from the same brand ("I have purchased many of the
    Adventure Challenge books", Amanda; Faith Martin owns couples 1 and 2, In
    Bed, mini dates and the advent calendar);
  - giving the book again ("our favorite gift to give to friends who get
    engaged").

## 6. Design lessons for a Quiz Couple book

### Do
1. **Design for a pace people actually keep.** Use undated pages, or pages
   dated by the reader, with "one page when you feel like it" or a weekly
   rhythm. Add a progress tracker instead of 365 dated days. Daily discipline
   is the main point of failure (Erin Cataldi, Troy Zaher, Velena).
2. **Answer separately, then reveal.** Make "write your answer, hide it, then
   compare" or "guess what your partner said" the default mechanic. Users
   arrive at it on their own (Velena, Ramona Mead), and it is Quiz Couple's
   own format (matching answers, scores).
3. **Give real writing room, and vary it.** Fast pages (checkboxes, one line,
   A/B choices) sit beside deep pages with 6 to 8 lines each. Some people find
   three lines inviting, wordy people find them too few.
4. **Keep the background light wherever people write.** Test with a
   ballpoint and a gel pen. Use heavy paper, and add a bookmark ribbon or a
   "where we are" marker if possible.
5. **Questions must be specific, varied, and checked for duplicates.** No
   near-duplicate questions, no filler, no "so basic, how would your partner
   not know", no dated pop-culture questions. Proofread the whole book against
   duplicates (Romane M. found a duplicated page).
6. **Inclusive by default.** Write "your partner" and "you two", never "his or
   her". Assume no gender, marriage, children or particular duration. Mark
   optional sections (kids, living together, long distance, older couples).
7. **Spice is opt-in and labelled.** Put it in a clearly marked section or a
   separate edition. Give a content note before heavy topics (infidelity,
   trust).
8. **For activity or bucket-list pages, tag each idea** with cost (free / € /
   €€), time, home or outdoors, season, and "works with kids". Prefer
   realistic everyday ideas over role-play, avoid repeated gimmicks
   (blindfolds) and anything unsafe or illegal, and adapt the ideas to France.
9. **Photo spaces in standard sizes** (instant-camera and 10x15 phone prints)
   with a few lines beside them. Tell buyers what to print.
10. **Plan for the reluctant partner.** One person can drive (a fill-in gift
    written by one partner), and the other can just answer out loud (quiz
    pages read aloud). Keep sessions short (Velena suggests about 10 minutes).
11. **Build for reading again and buying again.** Add "then and now" pages,
    "our answers next year" columns, a time-capsule page, and a Volume 2 or
    themed sequels. People already buy second copies and sequels.
12. **Make it ready to give, and on time.** A gift-looking cover, a
    dedication page, an early-order message before Valentine's Day and
    Christmas (print-on-demand lead times), and nothing that spoils the
    surprise on the outside of the package.
13. **Add real content, not only blank space.** The "steep for a book where you
    write most of the text" criticism is answered by quizzes with scoring and
    explanations, illustrations (the mascots), and ideas. This is my
    inference from the reviews, not a buyer quote.
14. **Help the giver.** In fill-in pages, put example answers or memory
    triggers next to the blanks. People search for "answer ideas", and filler
    prompts are what draw criticism.
15. **Make pages that look good on camera** (the reveal page, the score page,
    "our top 3"), because reveals get filmed. Keep the intimate pages private.

### Don't
- Don't use 365 dated daily pages that make people feel guilty for missing a
  day.
- Don't put dark full-bleed colour under the writing lines, and don't give
  2-3 lines to deep questions.
- Don't pad the book with near-duplicate or generic questions; a book that
  reads as AI-written will be noticed (the Authors Guild says AI is "driving
  a new surge of low-quality sham "books" on Amazon",
  https://authorsguild.org/news/ai-driving-new-surge-of-sham-books-on-amazon/).
- Don't assume a heterosexual, married couple with children.
- Don't mix spicy content into a general book without telling the buyer.
- Don't promise in the title what the inside doesn't do (the bucket-list
  confusion).
- Don't write activities that cost a lot, depend on location, are role-play,
  or are unsafe.
- Don't copy scratch-off on print-on-demand. It can't be printed there, and
  where it exists it fails (ripping, smearing, slow). Use alternatives: sealed
  "open when" pages, tear-out cards, stickers.
- Don't make a "long-term" book that a couple finishes in two days, or price
  it as a quick game if it is one.
