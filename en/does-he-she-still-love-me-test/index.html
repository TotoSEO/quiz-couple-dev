Does he or she still love me?

Twenty everyday situations to find out whether their feelings are still there, or whether something has drifted away. Instant result, and a few ideas for what comes next.

Free, confidential, instant result

Loading the test…

The doubt that settles in without a sound

The question doesn't arrive all at once. It comes in small touches, and one day it's there.

First come the details. An "I love you" that no longer comes on its own. Evenings side by side, each on your own screen. A holiday plan that has stayed vague for weeks. Taken one by one, these details mean nothing. Put together, they end up asking a question you don't dare say out loud: does he or she still love me?

This test is here to look at those details calmly. It doesn't hand down a verdict, nobody can read someone else's mind. But it puts the facts back in order: what they do, what they no longer do, and since when.

One thing to keep in mind before you answer: loving less intensely than at the start is not the same as no longer loving. Most couples go through flat periods, and many people wonder at that point whether they stay out of love or out of habit. The test tells the difference between a passion that has calmed down and a bond that is coming apart.

"The hardest part isn't hearing no. It's no longer knowing what he or she is thinking."

What reassures, what should alert you

The same daily life can be read two ways. This table sets side by side what you see in someone who still loves, and in someone who has drifted away.

You tell them about your dayThey listen and ask questionsThey look at their phone, or cut it short
An argumentYou make up, you talk about it again, even clumsilyThe silence lasts for days, or the argument is used to pull further away
Plans six months outThey talk about them with enthusiasm"We'll see", or the subject is avoided
Tender gesturesThey're there, without thinkingYou're always the one making them

What changes when someone stops loving

When feelings go, it rarely shows in big declarations. It shows in four very concrete places in everyday life.

The time you're given

Someone who loves finds time, even when they don't have any. They suggest something, move something around, come home a bit earlier. When the bond drifts, it's the opposite: work, friends, sport, everything comes first, and you get what's left. Careful, though, a very busy period exists in every life. What should alert you is that it lasts, and that it doesn't bother them.

Plans

This is often the clearest sign. Someone who loves you sees themselves with you, even in small things: a weekend in two months, a piece of furniture to buy, whose place for Christmas. Someone who doubts stays vague, and someone who no longer loves avoids the subject altogether. If you often hear "I don't know where I'll be", hear that sentence for what it is.

Tenderness and desire

Desire drops in every couple, that's not a signal in itself. Tenderness, on the other hand, leaves much later. A hand on your back in passing, a kiss for no reason, a cuddle in front of the TV: when all of that disappears at the same time as desire, and only you seem to miss it, there's something to look at.

Listening

You don't listen to someone out of politeness when you love them, you listen because you want to know. If your sentences fall into a void, if you have to repeat everything, if your worries are no longer remembered, it isn't a memory problem. Many couples in trouble first stopped really talking to each other, long before they stopped loving each other.

Loving less intensely is not the same as no longer loving

Before you conclude anything, the false alarms have to be ruled out. Four situations look like the end of feelings and aren't.

Routine

After a few years, you no longer look at each other the same way. That's normal. Routine puts attention to sleep, it doesn't kill love. If the gestures come back as soon as you step out of daily life, a weekend, an evening without the kids, the bond is there.

Exhaustion

A baby who won't sleep, a job that spills over, a sick parent, a move: someone who is exhausted has nothing left to give, and it has nothing to do with you. The test shows it well, actually. Tiredness lowers the gestures, not the listening or the plans.

A rough patch

A bereavement, or a depression settling in. The person withdraws into themselves, and you feel pushed aside. Here, it isn't love that's missing, it's the room to express it. It comes back when the person is doing better.

A real drift

When the four signs from the previous section have been there for months, and nothing comes back even outside daily life, the question is no longer "does he still love me?" but "is this coming to an end?". They're two different tests, and the second one takes over from the first.

What you can do starting tonight

A result, whatever it is, is useless if you keep it to yourself. Here's where to start, in order.

1. Observe for a week, without testing anything

This isn't about setting a trap, or "seeing whether he notices". Just watch what happens, and note what does you good and what you miss. A week is enough to separate a bad day from a trend.

2. Ask the question simply

Not "do you still love me?" at midnight after an argument. Rather, in a calm moment: "I feel like we're drifting apart, do you feel it too?". The wording matters: you're talking about the two of you, not about their fault.

3. Say what you miss

With precise examples. "I'd like us to have evenings just the two of us again", "I miss it when you don't ask how my day went". Someone who still loves hears that. Someone who no longer loves gets defensive, or irritated.

4. Look at the couple, not only at the other person

If you doubt their feelings, there's often a doubt about the couple itself. Taking a couple test together sometimes says more than ten conversations: you see where each of you stands, without one accusing the other.

If you're afraid of their reaction when you talk about the two of you, if you've been walking on eggshells for months, the question is no longer only about feelings. Look towards the toxic relationship or coercive control tests: they're other tests, with other answers.

And what if you're the one who no longer loves?

The question sometimes turns around. You wonder whether he still loves you and, deep down, you're no longer so sure you love him. That's not a shameful confession, it's an honest question, and it deserves its own answer.

Two tests handle it better than this one: finding out whether you're still in love for the feelings, and love or habit for what makes you stay. Take them before asking the other person. You talk better when you know what you feel yourself!

How this test works

Twenty everyday situations, four answers each time, from the most reassuring to the coldest. There's no right answer, there's yours. Answer about the last three months, not last week: a week can be bad for a thousand reasons.

Each answer counts for points, and some questions weigh more, the ones about plans, tenderness and listening, because they say the most. Your score places you on four levels, from "he or she loves you, and it shows" to "the bond has come apart". At each level, a reading and a way forward.

This test is a tool to see more clearly, not proof. Nobody knows someone else's feelings. If the result worries you, the only thing that matters next is the conversation.

The questions people ask

How do I know if he still loves me without asking him?
By watching what he does rather than what he says, over several weeks. The time he gives you, the plans he makes with you, the everyday tenderness and the way he listens to you: these four signs don't lie for long. But at some point you'll still have to ask him. No test replaces the conversation.
He doesn't say I love you anymore, does that mean he doesn't love me?
Not necessarily. Many people say "I love you" less over the years and show it differently, by looking after you, making plans, listening to you. The signal isn't the word disappearing. It's the word and the gestures disappearing at the same time.
My girlfriend has been distant for a few weeks, should I worry?
A few weeks is short. A busy period, tiredness, a worry she hasn't told you about yet can explain a distance. What should alert you is when it lasts for months, when nothing comes back even outside daily life, and when she doesn't seem bothered by that distance. When in doubt, ask her, simply.
He still loves me but we argue all the time, is that possible?
Yes, and it's even common. Arguments mean there's still something to defend. What's worrying is rather the opposite: no more arguments, no more reactions, nothing at all. After an argument, look above all at whether you make up or let it drag on.
Can you fall back in love with someone you stopped loving?
When love has gone out for good, it's rare. But most of the time, what we take for the end of feelings is a long period without attention. And there, yes, it often comes back as soon as you give yourselves time together again. That's exactly why the test separates the two cases.
Can this test tell me whether he's cheating on me?
No. A drift can have a hundred reasons, and an affair is only one of them. If that precise question is what's eating at you, there's a test on the signs of infidelity, which looks at other things.
