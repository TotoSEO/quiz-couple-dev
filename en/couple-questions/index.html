120 Questions for Couples to Ask Each Other

Questions sorted by theme to know each other better, strengthen your bond, and create moments of authentic exchange.

The 120 questions, sorted by theme

Here is the full list. Each theme has twenty-four questions, to read in order or to pick at random.

Questions to get to know each other better Questions about your plans and your future Questions about intimacy and feelings Funny and light questions Deep questions about values and life

Questions to get to know each other better

These dig underneath the obvious. We think we know someone because we know their habits; these twenty-four questions are about what shaped them.

  1. 1.Which decision from your past changed you the most, and did you realise it at the time?
  2. 2.What did your parents pass on to you without ever saying it out loud?
  3. 3.Which compliment genuinely lands with you, and which one makes you uncomfortable?
  4. 4.What were you certain of at twenty that you no longer believe at all?
  5. 5.What are you genuinely good at that almost nobody notices?
  6. 6.Which childhood memory comes back to you most often, for no particular reason?
  7. 7.What tires you out in other people, when it seems to bother no one else?
  8. 8.Which friendship shaped you the most, and where is it now?
  9. 9.In what kind of situation do you feel most like yourself?
  10. 10.Which criticism stuck with you enough that you still think about it?
  11. 11.What would you do with your days if money stopped being part of the equation?
  12. 12.Which trait did you inherit and are still trying to correct?
  13. 13.What do people get wrong about you, more or less systematically?
  14. 14.Which failure turned out to be the most useful, looking back?
  15. 15.When was the last time you changed your mind about something that mattered?
  16. 16.What makes you angry, when you wish it left you cold?
  17. 17.Which place makes you feel better the moment you arrive?
  18. 18.What have you never told anyone, not out of shame, but because the moment never came?
  19. 19.Which quality do you admire most, and don't have?
  20. 20.What reassures you when everything is going wrong?
  21. 21.Which habit have you picked up recently, and what set it off?
  22. 22.If you had to describe yourself in one sentence you would never use in a job interview, what would it be?
  23. 23.Which skill would you love to have, without ever having taken the time to learn it?
  24. 24.What has changed in you since we've been together, without my noticing?

Questions about your plans and your future

Couples rarely split over a fundamental disagreement; they split because it was never put into words. These questions put it into words, while there is still time to talk about it.

  1. 25.What does an ordinary day of our life five years from now look like, in your head?
  2. 26.What is the one thing you really don't want to see happen to us?
  3. 27.Where would you want to live if nothing kept us where we are?
  4. 28.What should we have done together before we turn fifty?
  5. 29.How do you picture us handling money in ten years?
  6. 30.What role do you want work to play in your life later on?
  7. 31.In twenty years, what would make you say we got our relationship right?
  8. 32.Is there a plan you set aside because of our relationship?
  9. 33.How present do you want our families to be in our life?
  10. 34.What frightens you most about growing old as a couple?
  11. 35.If we had to start over somewhere else tomorrow, would you go or stay?
  12. 36.What room do you want to leave for your friends in the years ahead?
  13. 37.How would you like us to make the big decisions?
  14. 38.What do you absolutely want to pass on to someone one day?
  15. 39.When would be the right moment for the next big step for us?
  16. 40.Is there something you expect from me that you have never actually asked for?
  17. 41.How do you want us to organise ourselves if one of us has to stop working?
  18. 42.Which habit would you like us to take up together and keep for a long time?
  19. 43.What should we stop putting off?
  20. 44.How would we know it's time to change something in our life?
  21. 45.Which compromise are you willing to make, and which one will you never make?
  22. 46.What would you want us to keep exactly as it is today?

Why ask questions as a couple?

Communication is the cornerstone of a fulfilling relationship. Asking each other questions helps maintain an authentic connection and avoid the routine that sometimes creeps into relationships. Even after years together, there are always new things to discover about each other.

Studies in relationship psychology show that couples who regularly discuss their emotions, desires, and plans develop a deeper mutual understanding. These exchanges strengthen complicity, create shared memories, and help you better understand your partner's needs, dreams, and concerns.

Asking your partner questions is also a way of showing genuine interest beyond day-to-day life. It's an act of active listening that nurtures trust and emotional intimacy. Whether you're at the beginning of your relationship or have been together for years, these moments of exchange are essential to keeping your relationship alive.

Our 120 questions are divided into 5 complementary themes, designed to cover all aspects of your relationship. Each category addresses a specific need and allows you to explore a different facet of your couple.

When to use these couple questions?

There is no bad moment. Some situations simply lend themselves to it better than others.

  • Early in a relationship

    In the first months you mostly learn facts: the job, the family, the tastes. These questions save time on what comes next, how the other person works when they are tired, annoyed or happy.

  • After several years together

    Curiosity fades because you think you've covered everything. That's almost always wrong: someone's answers at thirty are not the ones they gave at twenty-five. If you want to measure the gap, how much you still have in common can be tested too.

  • When the miles get involved

    Most long distance relationships die of a call that no longer says anything: the day, the weather, good night. One question per call is enough to break that pattern.

  • An evening with no ideas

    Rather than a screen each. Three questions and a drink fill an evening. If you'd rather have something livelier, there are also games for two.

  • When something is off

    An open question is easier to answer than a reproach. And sometimes what you're really asking is whether you're still there out of love or out of habit, which is a question in its own right.

How to use these questions effectively? Our tips

A few simple rules make the difference between a conversation and an interrogation.

  • Three to five questions, no more

    Past that, people answer quickly to get it over with. Better to stop on an answer that opens something than to run through fifteen.

  • Listen instead of preparing the next one

    The reflex is to pick your next question while the other is talking. Follow up instead: "and at that moment, what did you feel?"

  • Accept that some get skipped

    If a question makes someone uncomfortable, move on, no comment. Insisting turns the game into an exam.

  • Answer as well

    Whoever asks all the questions never gives anything away. The exchange only exists if both go through it.

Who are these questions for?

These 120 questions are not aimed at one particular kind of couple.

  • New couples

    They speed up a phase that usually takes months.

  • Long-settled couples

    They reopen a curiosity that routine had closed.

  • Long distance couples

    They fill calls that were running on empty.

  • Couples talking again

    After a hard period, they give a subject that isn't the conflict itself.

And if the real question is no longer curiosity but how solid the bond is, that can be looked at head on: you can check what makes a relationship healthy, or start again from the twenty scored questions of a couple test.

The proven benefits of couple communication

Research in psychology confirms what many couples intuit: communicating regularly and deeply is the best predictor of relationship longevity. Couples who maintain discussion rituals report significantly higher relationship satisfaction.

Concrete benefits include better conflict management (because you better understand each other's reactions), strengthened emotional intimacy, an increased ability to weather challenges together, and simply more moments of shared joy.

Our 120 questions are designed as a practical tool to integrate these moments of exchange into your daily life, without pressure or formality. Pick a random question, start the conversation, and let the magic happen.

Frequently asked questions about couple questions

How many questions should you ask in a row?
We recommend asking 3 to 5 questions per session to keep the exchange natural and avoid fatigue. The goal is to create a real dialogue, not a questionnaire.
Is this suitable for all couples?
Yes, these questions are designed for all couples, whether together for a few weeks or several years. The 5 categories allow you to choose the depth level suited to your situation.
Can these questions be used long-distance?
Absolutely! These questions are ideal for long-distance couples. You can ask them during a video call, by message, or even send each other a question a day to maintain the connection.
What to do if a question makes you uncomfortable?
Simply move to another question without pressure. Respecting each other's boundaries is essential for maintaining a climate of trust.
Do you have to answer all the questions?
No, choose the ones that speak to you and match the mood of the moment. What matters is the quality of the exchange, not the number of questions asked.
How often should you use these questions?
There's no strict rule. Some couples enjoy asking one question a day, others prefer a session of 5 questions on weekends. Find the rhythm that works for you.
Can these questions replace couple therapy?
No, these questions are a complementary communication tool. If you're going through serious difficulties, we recommend consulting a professional. Our questions can, however, be an excellent complement.

Take three tonight, not thirty. One good answer beats a list ticked off.
