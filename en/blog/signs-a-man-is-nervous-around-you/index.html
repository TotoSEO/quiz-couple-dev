The signs that show a man is nervous around a woman

Psychology

Quick summary

  • A nervous man gives himself away mainly through his hands, his mouth and his eyes.
  • Looking away is not a sign of lying or of disinterest: it is his brain freeing up space.
  • Nervousness on its own tells you nothing. What tells you something is that he stays and keeps the conversation going.
  • The worst reaction is to point it out. The best is to lower the stakes.
  • Side by side, walking or doing something, the nervousness drops on its own.

He laughs a bit too loudly. He cannot look at you for more than two seconds. He has been turning his glass around in his hands for ten minutes without drinking from it.

Here are the 12 signs that show a man is nervous around you, what is going on in his body at that moment, and what to do with all of it.

The 12 signs that give a nervous man away

None of these signs counts on its own. It is when you see three or four of them at once that there is no doubt left.

  1. He swallows a lot, and he drinks a lot: he swallows every thirty seconds, he takes another sip when he has just had one.
  2. He cannot hold your gaze: two seconds, then he looks away, then he comes back. It goes back and forth the whole time.
  3. He touches his face, his neck, the back of his head: he rubs his neck, runs a hand through his hair, pinches his nose. Usually without noticing.
  4. His hands are never empty: the glass, the coaster, his keys, his phone, the paper napkin he is reducing to confetti.
  5. He talks fast, and he talks a lot: he keeps going, he leaves no gaps, he answers questions you never asked.
  6. He laughs when nothing is funny: that little laugh at the end of a sentence, the one that goes with nothing.
  7. His voice goes up a notch: a little higher and a little tighter than when he talks to his mates.
  8. He goes red, often in patches: it starts on the neck and climbs to the ears, and he knows it, which does not help.
  9. His palms are damp: you catch him wiping them discreetly on his jeans before saying hello.
  10. A leg that jiggles, a foot that moves: sitting down, it shows immediately. Standing, he shifts his weight every ten seconds.
  11. He corrects himself constantly: "well, what I mean is", "no but you know what I mean", "sorry, I'm putting that badly".
  12. He checks his phone when nothing has happened: no notification, no call. He just needs a three-second break.

You will notice that almost all of it happens in three places: the hands, the mouth and the eyes. That is not a coincidence, and it explains itself rather well.

What is happening in his body at that moment

A situation that matters triggers the same machinery as a danger. The body goes on alert, adrenaline rises, the heart speeds up and blood heads for the muscles. It is the sympathetic nervous system taking over, and it does not tell the difference between a bear and a woman he likes.

The mouth: on alert, saliva changes: it gets thicker, and the mouth gets that unpleasant dry feeling. Hence the constant swallowing and the sip of water every two minutes. It is also why the voice tightens a little.

The hands: the sweat glands in the palms respond to emotion, not only to heat. Social stress alone is enough to set them off, and a man who knows it will spend his evening drying his hands. As for the gestures towards the face and the neck, those are self-soothing: we touch ourselves to calm down, the way we rub our eyes when we are tired.

The eyes: this is the sign that almost always gets read the wrong way. In 1998, Arthur Glenberg and his team published five experiments in Memory & Cognition on looking away. The result: the harder the question, the more people avert their eyes to answer it. And it is not embarrassment, the researchers checked that point specifically. It is useful: looking away cuts out part of what is coming in from outside, and frees up room to think.

In other words, when he is talking to you while staring at the bar, it is not that he is avoiding you. Often it is that he is looking for what to say, and your face takes up too much bandwidth for him to manage it while looking at you.

The blushing: that one stands apart, because it serves no purpose at all. Darwin gave it the final chapter of The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals, in 1872, and called it "the most peculiar and the most human of all expressions". No other animal blushes. It appears in people blind from birth, so it is not learned, and it fires for one reason only: thinking about what others think of us. It is the most honest sign on the list, precisely because it cannot be faked.

Is he nervous because he likes me?

Honest answer: nervousness on its own proves nothing. A man can be nervous because he finds you attractive, but also because he is shy with everyone, because he has had a rough day, or because he does not like groups.

What tells you something is what he does with his nervousness. Here is how to sort it out.

What you noticeWhich way it leans
He is nervous, but he stays and keeps the conversation goingHe likes you. He is afraid of getting it wrong, so he cares about getting it right.
He is nervous and he cuts it short, glancing towards the doorHe is uncomfortable, and mostly he wants it to end.
He is nervous with you and relaxed with everyone elseIt is you. Watch him talk to someone else, the difference jumps out.
He is like that with everyoneIt is his shyness, and it says nothing about you.
He is nervous and he asks you questions about yourselfGood sign. He wants to know you despite the discomfort.
He is nervous and he mostly talks about himselfHe is managing his image more than he is interested in you. See how he is next time.

There is also the man who is so nervous that he cancels at the last minute. It is not that he does not want to come, it is that the pressure got too high. If that happens to you, what he does in the minutes that follow will tell you far more than the cancellation itself.

And some men will never say it first. A man can be attentive, nervous, around for months, and still keep his feelings to himself while he waits for a sign from you. With the very shy ones, that is actually the most common scenario.

How to react in that situation, as a woman

You have three options in front of you, and only one of them is bad. Let us start with that one.

Whatever you do, don't point it out

"Are you okay, you've gone all red?", "are you stressed or something?", "relax!". Those three lines come from a good place and do the exact opposite of what they are trying to do.

Blushing, as we saw, fires when you think about what others think of you. Telling him he is red therefore makes him redder, mechanically. Same with the rest: naming his nervousness doubles it, because he discovers that it shows.

Act as if you had noticed nothing. That does not mean putting on an act, just not shining a light on it.

Change the position rather than the subject

This is the most useful tip on the page. When someone is nervous, the instinct is to look for another topic of conversation. It does not work, because the problem is not the topic, it is the face-to-face.

A face-to-face is two people looking at each other, with nothing to keep the hands busy and no escape route for the eyes. It is the hardest possible setup for someone who is nervous.

So change it. Suggest walking a bit. Go and get the drinks together. Step outside for some air. Side by side, everything gets easier: the hands have something to do, the eyes are allowed to look ahead, and the silences stop weighing anything. You will often meet a completely different person five minutes later.

If you are stuck face to face and there is nothing to be done, hand him questions that are easy to catch: his worst night out, the series he is embarrassed to love, that kind of thing.

Use his nerves to catch him out (without being mean)!

Now for the fun part. His nervousness gives you an advantage, and you are perfectly entitled to use it. The rule is simple: tease, never mock.

The difference is who is laughing. If you laugh at him, it is unkind and he shuts down. If you laugh with him, you have just taken the pressure off both of you, and that is exactly what he needed.

A few lines that work.

  • The mock accusation: "are you avoiding my eyes on purpose or do I scare you?", said with a smile. You name the thing, but as a game, and you give him the chance to answer "a bit of both". That is when you have won.
  • The disguised compliment: "that's cute, you're shredding your napkin into little bits." He will laugh, he will stop, and he will have understood that it does not bother you.
  • The fake dare: "I bet you can't look at me for five seconds without looking away." You turn the thing that blocks him into a game, and a game is not frightening.
  • Honesty, shared: "for what it's worth, I'm a bit nervous too." He is no longer the only nervous one, there are two of you, and you have just halved the pressure.

That slightly teasing, slightly forward tone is what wins most men over, far more than perfect politeness. A woman who dares to joke with him is a woman who is not afraid of him, and he feels it straight away. And it is often from that kind of exchange, a bit clumsy and not at all rehearsed, that the good stories start.

📌 Nervous is not a flaw, it is information

A man who is never thrown, who has the right line for everything on the first evening and who showers you with compliments, is not necessarily better. Sometimes it is the start of a pattern that has a name, that of the one who does too much, too fast. Awkwardness is the opposite of a bad sign: it means the moment matters to him.

A few tips to put him at ease

Nothing complicated here, and you do not have to carry the evening on your own. These five things are usually enough.

  • Talk a little more slowly than he does: rhythm is contagious. If he talks fast and you slow down, he slows down with you within two minutes, without even noticing.
  • Drop the high-stakes questions at the start: "what's your biggest dream" in the first five minutes is unmanageable for someone who is stressed. Start with questions that are easy to answer.
  • Let silences sit without filling them: if you rush to fill every gap, you teach him that gaps are a problem. A calm three-second silence relaxes him more than one more sentence.
  • Tell him something about you, including something that went wrong: one slightly embarrassing story from your side, and he understands he does not have to be perfect. It is the fastest way to bring the pressure down.
  • Give him one clear piece of information at the end: "that was nice, let's do it again." One sentence, and he stops wondering whether he ruined everything. It is the best gift you can give him that evening.

And if after two or three times he is still in the same state, it is no longer really nerves. First-time nervousness wears off fast once someone feels safe, and if it does not wear off, something else is going on.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if he is nervous or just shy? Watch him with other people. A shy man is like that with everyone. A man who is nervous because of you is relaxed with others and stiffens when you arrive.

A man is nervous around me, does that mean he is in love? Not necessarily in love, but there is something at stake. You are clearly not a matter of indifference to him, or there would be nothing to get wrong.

Why does he avoid my eyes when I talk to him? Most often because he is looking for what to say. Looking away frees up room to think, and that is documented. It is neither lying nor disinterest.

Why does he laugh all the time with me? Laughter is the fastest way to release tension. If he laughs when nothing is funny, that is discharge, not mockery.

Does it wear off with time? Yes, and fairly quickly. Count on two or three meetings. Beyond that, if nothing shifts, it is more than simple nerves.

How do I tell nervousness from discomfort? The nervous one stays and keeps things going. The uncomfortable one shortens everything, looks elsewhere and finds a reason to leave.

Can a confident man be nervous? Yes, and it is common. Being sure of yourself at work or with friends protects you from nothing when you really like someone.

Can I tell him I noticed? Later, yes, and in a teasing tone. In the moment, no: saying it only adds pressure.
