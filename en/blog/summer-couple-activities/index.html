10 Summer Activities to Do as a Couple

Quick summary

  • The best summer activities for two are rarely the most expensive or the most organized.
  • Rediscovering your own city (shops, museums, monuments) costs almost nothing and works surprisingly well.
  • A tablecloth, some crisps and three hours in the grass are often worth more than an outing planned a month ahead.
  • Constraints create the memory: 27 photos maximum, an itinerary decided by a coin toss, a market with no shopping list.
  • Plan the logistics (water, opening hours, weather) but not the schedule: it's the improvisation that makes the day memorable.

In summer, we have time, daylight until 10pm and... often zero ideas. So we redo the same terrace, the same film, the same walk around the same park. Yet the best summer couple activities are almost never the expensive ones or the ones that take three weeks to plan. They're simple things, a bit silly sometimes, that we just never do. Here are ten, tested and approved at home.

What to do as a couple in summer? 10 ideas

The 10 activities we suggest:

  1. Try the shops in your city you've never set foot in
  2. Lay a tablecloth on the grass and do nothing else
  3. Play tourist in your own town: museums, monuments, old stones
  4. Follow a river by bike until you find a swimming spot
  5. Get up before the sun and have breakfast outside
  6. One disposable camera, 27 photos, a single day
  7. The market with no list, then cook whatever you found
  8. The heads-or-tails day: the coin decides the route
  9. Sleep outside without going on holiday
  10. The questions evening, outside, while it's still mild

1. Try the shops in your city you've never set foot in

Take thirty seconds and count: how many different restaurants do you really go to? Three? Four? We live in a city for years, we walk past the same bakery, the same café, the same little street food stand every day, and we never step inside. We say "we should try that" and we go back to the usual spot.

The idea is to block out a whole afternoon with one single rule: only places you've never been. An example route that works really well:

  • 3pm: a bakery, the one you walk past all the time. One cake each, and you swap halves.
  • 3:45pm: a café, but not the one next door. Walk ten minutes to find a new one.
  • 4:30pm: a walk, with no destination, through a neighbourhood you never cross.
  • 6:30pm: street food. The weird kebab place, the bao truck, the tiny six-seat Thai canteen.

The budget comes to around €25 to €35 for two, and you go home with two or three addresses that will become your new habits. It's honestly the best value activity on this list.

2. Lay a tablecloth on the grass and do nothing else

This one, everyone sees other people doing it and nobody does it. We come across those people lying in a park, we think "oh that's nice"... and we go back home.

So try it, really. Grab a tablecloth (or an old sheet, that works too), and bring:

  • A bag of crisps and some sweets, we're not going to pretend to like raw veggies
  • A nice cold bottle of soda, or sparkling water if you're being sensible
  • A book each
  • A small speaker, at low volume out of respect for your lawn neighbours
  • Sunscreen, because you fall asleep and regret it

No program, no end time. You'll read for twenty minutes, chat, doze off, rediscover that silence together is pleasant when it isn't awkward. If the mood turns chatty, a slightly silly quiz to play out loud extends the moment perfectly. We guarantee these two or three hours will be among the best of your summer.

3. Play tourist in your own town: museums, monuments, old stones

When we go on holiday, we do everything: the cathedral, the history museum, the viewpoint, the 2pm guided tour. And at home? Nothing. Sometimes we live fifteen minutes' walk from a monument the whole world comes to see, and we've never gone up it.

With Mathieu, we lived seven years in Strasbourg before going up to the top of the cathedral. Seven years! We walked past it every week. The day we finally did it, it took us twenty minutes to come back down because we couldn't bring ourselves to leave.

A few concrete ideas: the municipal museum (often free or under €6), the highest viewpoint open to the public, a guided tour of the historic centre by the tourist office, or the heritage open days. In many cities, public museums are free one Sunday a month: ask around, it changes everything on a tight budget.

The real benefit isn't cultural, it's elsewhere: you'll look at your own city as if you'd just arrived. And that puts a bit of wonder back into a daily life that doesn't have much left.

4. Follow a river by bike until you find a swimming spot

Forget the well-known lakes and the packed leisure parks where you pay €8 to lay your towel against someone else's. Grab the bikes, find a river or a canal, and follow it upstream. That's it.

After an hour of easy pedalling, you'll almost always end up stumbling on something: a small pebble beach, a calm stretch of water, an old wooden jetty. These places exist everywhere, they're just on no map and nobody talks about them. That's exactly what makes them good.

Two precautions, because we care about you: these swimming spots aren't supervised, so test the depth before jumping and watch out for currents, even when the surface looks calm. And check the signs, some areas are off-limits for good reasons (dam upstream, boat traffic, water quality).

Bring a towel, water, and leave the phones in the bag. You won't turn them back on.

5. Get up before the sun and have breakfast outside

Everyone watches the sunset. Almost nobody watches the sunrise. Yet it's the same show, calmer, with a light you'll never see any other way... and a completely empty city.

The trick is to prepare everything the night before: thermos of coffee, pastries bought the evening before, a blanket, alarm set. In the morning you have nothing to decide, you get up and you go. Aim for a slightly high spot, a hill, a bridge, an accessible rooftop, a clearing at the edge of a field.

Yes, in summer that means getting up around 5am. That's the price. And it's also what makes the moment special: the two of you are doing something nobody else is doing at that hour. You'll be back by 8am, you'll still have the whole day ahead of you, and you'll already have a story to tell.

6. One disposable camera, 27 photos, a single day

A disposable costs between €12 and €20. Twenty-seven photos, not one more. No screen, no editing, no "wait, let's take it again."

The rules we apply:

  • One whole day, one camera, you pass it back and forth
  • Nobody says "pose", the candid shots are always the best
  • No counting how many are left after the tenth one
  • The last two photos are saved for the end of the day, no matter what

Then comes the part we love: the ten-day wait before developing. You've forgotten half the shots, you don't know which ones came out well, and the day you pick up the prints, you relive the day a second time. Out of 27, eight will be blurry, five will be flops, and three you'll frame.

Compare that to the 400 photos on your camera roll that you'll never open again.

7. The market with no list, then cook whatever you found

Head to the market on a Saturday morning, with no menu in mind. The rule: each of you picks three products, without consulting the other, and everything has to be used in lunch.

It's a great revealer, actually. You'll discover that your partner buys things you'd never have picked, and that you have to negotiate to turn it into a coherent dish. The best meals are born from these absurd constraints.

Mathieu came back one day with figs, chorizo and a fresh goat cheese. I thought it was a lost cause. We've been making it every week since.

Budget around €20 to €30 for two, and plan to eat outside: on a balcony, in a garden, or even at the camping table unfolded in the courtyard. Summer variation if you're feeling motivated: pick-your-own at a farm in the morning (strawberries, cherries, apricots depending on the season), and turn the harvest into jam or a tart in the afternoon.

8. The heads-or-tails day: the coin decides the route

The principle is daft and that's exactly why it works. You leave the house, on foot or by car, and at every intersection, you flip a coin. Heads, you go left. Tails, you go right.

Set two safeguards before leaving: a maximum duration (45 minutes of travel, for example) and a fallback rule if you hit a dead end or a motorway. Once you reach the end, you stop wherever you are. You eat at the first decent place, walk a bit, and head home.

What does it produce? You discover villages, country roads and places you'd never have chosen on purpose. And above all, nobody carries the burden of "finding a good idea" anymore. The coin decides, so nobody can be disappointed by the other's choice. That detail is worth more than it seems.

9. Sleep outside without going on holiday

A night outside doesn't mean driving 300 km to go camping. A garden, a terrace, a wide enough balcony, a courtyard: that's plenty.

What you really need:

  • An air mattress or two thick sleeping mats, the ground is harder than you think
  • A duvet, because it's 14 °C at 4am even in July
  • A tarp or a mat underneath, the dew soaks through everything
  • A mosquito net or citronella, otherwise the night will be short
  • Zero screens

You'll talk until 2am. It's mechanical: lying side by side in the dark, no TV, no notifications, the conversation drifts somewhere else. These are often the most honest talks of the year. And if you have a clear sky, look out for the Perseids around August 12, it's the annual peak of shooting stars and it's visible from almost the entire northern hemisphere.

10. The questions evening, outside, while it's still mild

It's 9:30pm, it's still 24 °C, you're on the balcony or in the garden. It's the best slot of the summer to talk about things you never talk about.

The format that works: a drink, two chairs facing each other, and a series of questions a bit deeper than the everyday ones. You take turns, each answers honestly, and above all nobody comments on the other's answer before it's finished. That last rule changes everything.

If the mood is more lighthearted, go for some completely absurd "would you rather" questions and let the conversation drift on its own. We've noticed that within an hour, it almost always tips into something serious without anyone deciding to.

An hour of that is worth three evenings in front of a series. And it costs nothing.

The 4 mistakes that turn a good idea into a wasted day

Overplanning. A schedule timed to the half-hour is a guarantee that the slightest delay becomes a source of tension. Plan the logistics (water, opening hours, weather), never the schedule.

Going out at the worst hours. Between noon and 4pm in July, everything is more of a pain: the heat, the queues, the tiredness. Shift your outings to early morning or late afternoon, the difference in the overall mood is huge.

Keeping your phone in your hand. You don't need to leave it at home. Put it on airplane mode and only take it out for photos. A couple scrolling side by side on a blanket isn't an activity, it's two solitudes.

Waiting for "the right moment". It's the most common and most costly mistake. We put it off to next weekend, then the one after, and by the end of August we've done nothing. The right moment is next Saturday.

One signal not to ignore, too: if your outings systematically turn into pure logistics ("when are we leaving?", "did you grab the keys?") and never into anything else, it's not an activity problem. It's often the symptom of a conversation that has quietly grown thinner without anyone noticing, and that's worked on separately.

What if one of you doesn't feel like anything?

It happens, and it's not necessarily serious. But it's worth understanding why before pushing.

In most cases, "I don't feel like it" isn't about the activity itself. It's about the effort of going out, getting ready, managing. When someone is fried by their job, a five-step proposal feels like an extra load, not like relaxation. The right reaction isn't to insist, it's to lower the bar: the tablecloth in the park and the questions evening take fifteen minutes of prep, no more.

Second angle, less pleasant but real: sometimes you simply don't want the same things. One wants movement, the other wants calm, and every outing becomes a disguised negotiation. There's nothing abnormal about that, as long as you name it. Alternating honestly (today it's your idea, next time mine, and the other doesn't grumble) solves 80% of the problem.

And if you feel like you're constantly suggesting things that miss the mark, test how well you really know their tastes. The results are sometimes stinging... and very instructive.

It took us a while to accept it, with Mathieu: a good couple activity isn't the one that looks nicest in a photo. It's the one where we're both happy to be there.

Take one from the list. Just one. Do it this weekend, without waiting for the perfect window that won't come. And if you like it, it'll probably become your thing.
