100 questions to ask your boyfriend, from light to the big conversations

Relationship
Published on August 25, 2026 | Thomas | 10 min read |

Quick summary

  • 100 questions sorted into 7 themes, from the easiest to the deepest.
  • The right way to use them: two or three per moment, not an interrogation.
  • Answer every question you ask him too, it's an exchange, not a quiz.
  • The questions about the future and about fears deserve a calm moment, one to one.
  • Want something more playful? These questions also exist as a quiz to take together.

You can spend an entire evening together and exchange three useful sentences: "what are we eating", "did you take the keys", "you alright?". Relax, it isn't a sign your relationship is in trouble. It's just what happens to every couple. After a while, logistics end up eating the conversation.

Good questions are a shortcut back to the conversations you've lost the reflex for. Not to play journalist, mind you. Just to find again that early stage where you could talk for hours without noticing the time...

So here are 100 questions to ask your boyfriend, ordered from the lightest to the deepest. Pick according to the moment: some work between two tube stops, others deserve a sofa, a real evening and no phone within reach. And there's a good chance two or three answers will surprise you, even after years.

Before you start: three rules that make it work

A handful at a time: Two or three questions in an evening is a conversation. Thirty in a row is an interrogation. The list is deliberately long: it's meant to last weeks, not one sitting.

Answer them yourself too: The rule of the game is exchange. If you ask him his biggest fear, be ready to tell yours... That's often exactly where the conversation really takes off.

And welcome the answer, whatever it is: If an answer surprises you or stings a little, you're allowed to dig. Not to plead. A question asked to trap someone never teaches you anything real, and you know that already.

One last thing: as the answers come, one question will inevitably surface, which of you two knows the other best? Save it for the end, it settles better with points on the board.

To know him better (1-15)

The foundations. Even after years together, some answers are going to surprise you.

  1. What do you wish people asked you more often?
  2. What are you proudest of that you never say out loud?
  3. What drains your energy, and what recharges it?
  4. Which moment from your childhood made you who you are today?
  5. What do people usually get wrong about you?
  6. Do you feel ahead, behind, or exactly where you wanted to be in life?
  7. Which habit would you like to lose, and which one do you want to keep forever?
  8. What makes you laugh every time, even on a bad day?
  9. Which compliment has stayed with you the longest?
  10. What were you doing at 15 that you never talk about?
  11. If you could master one thing overnight, what would it be?
  12. What's the hardest decision you've ever made?
  13. Do you prefer comfort or space when things go badly?
  14. Which object would you keep if you had to leave everything in five minutes?
  15. What have you been putting off for far too long?

About the two of you and your story (16-30)

The ones that bring memories back, and get you talking about "us" without turning it into a relationship review...

  1. At exactly what moment did you think this was going to get serious?
  2. What did you think of me the very first time?
  3. What's your favourite memory of us?
  4. What do I do that reassures you without me knowing?
  5. What's the kindest thing I've ever done for you?
  6. If you had to sum up our relationship in three words, which ones?
  7. What did we do at the beginning that we should do again?
  8. Which trip together marked you most, and why that one?
  9. What do you say about me when I'm not there?
  10. What did you almost give up for me without telling me?
  11. Which song makes you think of us?
  12. What have you learned about yourself since we've been together?
  13. What was our best improvised evening?
  14. What surprised you most as you got to know me?
  15. If we replayed our first date, what would you change?

This set has a known side effect: one question calls up ten more. On the evenings when the memory box swings wide open, a reserve of 120 questions for couples is far from a luxury.

Funny and light (31-45)

Zero stakes here. Just enough to laugh a bit, and discover his most absurd theories along the way.

  1. What's your strangest theory, the one you genuinely defend?
  2. What have you searched on your phone that you'd never admit out loud?
  3. You win a million, what do you do in the first ten minutes?
  4. Which animal would suit you best on a Monday morning?
  5. What's your worst haircut memory?
  6. If your life had a film title, what would it be?
  7. Which useless superpower would you like to have?
  8. Which habit of mine do you find ridiculous and adorable at once?
  9. How long would you survive in a horror film, honestly?
  10. Which dish would you defend to the death even though everyone hates it?
  11. What's the worst excuse you've invented to cancel something?
  12. If we swapped lives for a day, what would scare you most?
  13. How old would you say your brain is, right now?
  14. Which trend did you follow completely before feeling ashamed of it?
  15. Would you rather be able to sing or to dance?

You'll notice he defends his absurd theories with disconcerting seriousness; that's exactly what makes the dilemmas in a would you rather for couples impossible to settle.

Love and feelings (46-60)

The ones that get him talking about what he feels... which, let's be honest, he doesn't always put into words spontaneously.

  1. How do you know you love someone?
  2. What makes you feel loved, concretely?
  3. Would you say you fall in love fast or slowly?
  4. Which gesture counts more for you than words?
  5. What should love bring, in your view: calm or intensity?
  6. Which proof of love has moved you most in your life?
  7. What don't you dare ask me, for fear of my reaction?
  8. When do you feel closest to me?
  9. What do you miss when we don't see each other?
  10. How do you show you're upset without saying it?
  11. What is a beautiful love story, to you?
  12. What have your past relationships taught you about yourself in love?
  13. Do you think we choose who we fall in love with?
  14. What could I do more often that would do you good?
  15. If you had to say I love you without words, how would you do it?

And to find out whether he loves you, what do you ask?

No magic question will make someone say "I love you" if they don't mean it. But the answers to questions 46 to 60 paint a very clear picture: someone who invests in the conversation, who remembers, who projects forward, is answering you far beyond words. And if the doubt persists despite his answers, what you need isn't one more question, it's a way of reading: whether he secretly loves you shows up in behaviour, not in declarations.

A bit deeper: values, fears and convictions (61-80)

Save these for a calm moment. This is the set that turns an ordinary evening into a conversation you'll remember.

📌 The moment matters as much as the question

This set isn't for the doorway on the way out. A car journey, a walk, an evening without screens: deep conversations hate sitting face to face and love two people looking in the same direction.

  1. What frightens you most, not as danger, but as a life wasted?
  2. What would you never forgive?
  3. Which value would you most want to pass on?
  4. What genuinely makes you angry about the world?
  5. Do you think people can change deeply, or only adjust?
  6. Which failure ended up doing you a favour?
  7. What would you like your father or your mother to understand about you?
  8. Who are you willing to cry in front of?
  9. What do you do when you feel lost?
  10. Which conversation have you been avoiding for years, and with whom?
  11. What would you want people to say about you at 80?
  12. Do you believe in fate, in chance, or a mixture of both?
  13. Which period of your life would you relive, and which one never?
  14. What gives you the sense that your life has meaning?
  15. What advice would you give the you of ten years ago?
  16. What do you still carry from a past relationship, for better or worse?
  17. What's your relationship with money, and who did you inherit it from?
  18. What would make you say no to a dream: fear or reason?
  19. What have you changed your mind about in recent years?
  20. What have you never told anyone, not even me?

Question 80 sometimes opens sensitive doors: that's the register of intimate questions for couples, where we go further on that ground, with the care it deserves.

Future and plans (81-90)

You don't need to wait for a proposal to find out whether your trajectories match. Honestly, better these questions too early than too late.

  1. Where do you see yourself in five years, and how am I in that picture?
  2. What's non-negotiable in your future life?
  3. What's your definition of a successful life as a couple?
  4. What should we test together before committing further?
  5. City, countryside, abroad: where do you see yourself for good?
  6. Which project would you like us to start this year?
  7. What do you want to do before you're 40, whatever happens?
  8. How do you picture our weekends in ten years?
  9. What would make you say yes to a big decision: the desire or the right timing?
  10. If we could keep only one shared dream, which would you keep?

Number 84 is a trap: people think the answer is "nothing", then discover they never really checked whether they were ready to live together. Better to find that out before the boxes than after.

Over the phone or long distance (91-100)

Ten questions that work perfectly out loud or by message, for long-distance couples or evenings apart.

  1. What would you be doing right now if I were next to you?
  2. What was the first thing you thought this morning?
  3. Tell me one detail from your day you wouldn't have told anyone else.
  4. What did you miss today, apart from me obviously?
  5. Would you rather have a three-hour call or three hundred messages?
  6. Which photo on your phone best describes your week to me?
  7. What do we do first the next time we see each other?
  8. What would you like us to get better at, at a distance?
  9. What are you listening to right now that I should listen to too?
  10. If I turned up at yours in an hour, what would you change about your evening?

At a distance, the real risk isn't running out of subjects, it's that every exchange starts to sound the same; a quiz designed for long-distance couples, each on your own screen, breaks that routine better than a third video call in a row.

And after question 100?

If you've got this far, you've understood the principle: it isn't the questions that matter, it's the habit of asking them. Couples who last aren't the ones who know everything about each other... they're the ones who keep discovering each other.

So keep this list somewhere, and bring it back out in a month. Compare the answers in a year, too: some will have changed, and those changes are exactly the conversations you don't want to miss.

To know him betterA journey, a queue, a Sunday with no plans
About the two of youDinner for two, with no screen on the table
Funny and lightWhen the mood is good and nobody expects anything
Love and feelingsAt night, in the dark, when words come out more easily
Future and plansCalmly, never in the middle of an argument
Over the phoneWhen the call runs dry and there are twenty minutes left

You'll notice something else, by the way. Asking him questions makes him want to ask you some. And that's often how the balance comes back.

And the day you want to check everything you've just learned about each other... you know where to prove it.
