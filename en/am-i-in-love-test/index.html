Are you really in love?

20 questions to tell love from a crush, and finally know what you feel.

  1. On an ordinary day, how often do you think about this person?

    • Rarely, only when something reminds me of them.
    • Fairly often, without it taking over.
    • Several times a day, and always with pleasure.
    • Constantly. They are there in the background even when I am doing something else.

  2. When you are with them, what happens to you?

    • Nothing in particular, I am perfectly relaxed.
    • I am glad to see them, the way I am with a good friend.
    • My heart speeds up and I smile without deciding to.
    • Everything gets sharper: the colours, the passing time, whatever I am feeling.

  3. Their name appears on your phone. What is your first reaction?

    • I reply when I have time, without thinking about it much.
    • It makes me happy, like any nice message.
    • I drop what I am doing to read it straight away.
    • A little jolt in my chest before I have even opened the message.

  4. They go away for a week without getting in touch. What do you feel?

    • Nothing special, the week goes by like any other.
    • I miss them a bit, like a close friend.
    • A real absence, and I wait impatiently for them to come back.
    • An emptiness that is hard to fill, as if part of my daily life had gone dark.

  5. Something significant happens to you. Who do you want to tell?

    • Several people, they are not the first who comes to mind.
    • Them among others, with no particular priority.
    • Them first, almost every time.
    • Them, and the event feels unfinished until I have told them about it.

  6. Their flaws: do you see them?

    • I do not know them well enough to have noticed any.
    • I would rather not look at them, they would spoil the picture I have.
    • I see them, some irritate me, but they change nothing about what I feel.
    • I know them all, and I care about this person with those flaws, not in spite of them.

  7. You learn they are very happy with someone else. Your reaction?

    • Honestly, it would not affect me much.
    • A pang, quickly forgotten.
    • It would hurt for quite a while.
    • It would devastate me, even though deep down I would still want them to be happy.

  8. How much room does this person take up in your schedule?

    • None, I see them when our paths cross.
    • I am glad when we meet, but I do not arrange things around it.
    • I often rearrange things so that we can see each other.
    • I move important things for them, and I do it without hesitating.

  9. What do you most want to share with them?

    • Good times, nothing more than that.
    • Conversations, because they are interesting.
    • A lot, from the dull everyday to future plans.
    • My life, including the parts I show almost nobody.

  10. Do you ever picture a future with them?

    • Never, the question does not come up.
    • A few vague ideas, without really believing in them.
    • Yes, often, and thinking about it feels good.
    • Yes, and not only the good moments: I picture us getting through hard ones too.

  11. What do the people around you say when you talk about them?

    • Nothing in particular, I do not mention them often.
    • That they sound nice, and that is all.
    • That they hear me say their name a lot.
    • That my face changes when I talk about them, and they noticed before I did.

  12. How do you feel after spending time with them?

    • The same as before, no better and no worse.
    • In a good mood, like after any nice evening.
    • Recharged, wanting it to happen again soon.
    • Lighter and steadier at once, as if everything had got a little simpler.

  13. A long silence settles between the two of you. How does it feel?

    • Awkward, I start looking for something to say.
    • It depends on the day and the mood.
    • Comfortable, we do not need to fill the gaps.
    • It is one of my favourite things about being with them.

  14. What do you know about what they are going through right now?

    • Not much, we do not talk about that sort of thing.
    • The broad outline, what they tell everyone.
    • What is on their mind at the moment, because I ask them.
    • What frightens them and what they dream of, including what they rarely say out loud.

  15. How far would you go to help them, expecting nothing back?

    • As far as I would for anyone, no further.
    • A little further than for most people.
    • A long way, as long as it stays reasonable for me.
    • Very far, and it would feel like a pleasure rather than a sacrifice.

  16. Are you yourself around them?

    • Yes, but only because there is not much at stake for me.
    • I look after my image a little, nothing excessive.
    • I catch myself playing a part to please them.
    • I am myself, unflattering parts included, and it feels natural.

  17. Do you ever compare them with other people you have been drawn to?

    • They do not particularly stand out.
    • They have something extra, though nothing striking.
    • They eclipse the others by a long way right now.
    • The comparison does not even occur to me, they are simply in a category of their own.

  18. Do you remember your first real conversation with them?

    • Not really, it happened without my paying attention.
    • Vaguely, the setting more than the content.
    • Yes, quite well, it was a good moment.
    • In detail: the place, what they wore, what they said and what I felt.

  19. You are told this relationship will stay purely a friendship, for good.

    • That suits me perfectly, it already is one.
    • A bit of a shame, but I would live with it easily.
    • It would disappoint me a lot and I would need time.
    • It would be a real loss to grieve, even though I would choose to keep them in my life.

  20. If you had to tell them how you feel tomorrow, what would you feel?

    • Nothing to tell, there are no feelings of that kind.
    • Awkwardness, mainly because I am not sure what I feel.
    • Enormous fear, but the urge to do it anyway.
    • Relief at finally saying out loud something I already know.

Why this question is so hard to settle

If you are looking for an « am I in love » test, something is escaping you. That is entirely normal: nobody teaches us to recognise our own feelings. We know how to describe what we feel for a friend, a brother, a colleague, but as soon as love is involved the vocabulary blurs.

The confusion almost always comes from the same place: intensity. We assume that the stronger it is, the more it must be love. Yet the beginning of a story produces spectacular symptoms, a racing heart, obsessive thoughts, wrecked sleep, which look a lot like love without necessarily being it. Those signals say one thing for certain: this person matters. They do not yet say whether it is a crush or a lasting attachment.

That is exactly what this test tries to separate. It does not measure the intensity of what you feel but its nature: do you love this person, or the picture you have of them? Do you miss their presence, or is it the waiting that thrills you? Twenty questions later you will have a score out of 60 and a clear verdict.

The 7 signs that you are in love

No single sign is enough on its own, and the most telling one is not the one you would expect. Here are the ones the test weighs up, from the most obvious to the most decisive.

1

They occupy your thoughts unbidden

You think about this person several times a day without deciding to. Their name catches your attention in a conversation. It is the best known sign, but also the least reliable: plain attraction produces exactly the same effect.

2

They are the first person you want to tell

When something happens to you, good or bad, they are the one you want to tell first. That reaction says far more than butterflies: it means this person has become your reference point.

3

Their absence leaves a gap

A week without news does not leave you indifferent. Note the nuance: the missing that belongs to love is calm and steady, whereas anxious missing is something else and points to waiting or insecurity.

4

You see their flaws and they change nothing

Here is the sign that really separates the two. As long as the person seems perfect to you, you love an image. The day you know their failings, some of them irritate you, and your feelings hold anyway, it is love.

5

You want their happiness, even without you

The idea of them being happy pleases you, including when that happiness does not run through you. It is the most demanding criterion, the one that separates love from the wish to possess.

6

You are yourself around them

You are no longer playing a part or monitoring your image. Paradoxically, that letting go comes later than passion: at the start we try to please, later we dare to be ourselves.

7

Silence together is comfortable

Being able to say nothing together without awkwardness is an excellent marker of real intimacy. As long as a lull makes you uneasy, the stakes still outweigh the bond.

How to read your score

Each question carries a weight matching how serious it is, and each answer a value matching its rank. The total runs from 0 to 205 points. The most intense answers are not always the ones that score highest: idealising someone or playing a part to please them is worth fewer points than knowing them with their flaws. That is deliberate, and it is what separates love from a crush.

Love or plain attraction: telling them apart

The two feel remarkably alike from the inside, especially in the first weeks. Yet five criteria are enough to separate them, and none of them is about intensity.

What to look atAttractionLove
What you knowAn appealing picture, still blurry.A real person, flaws included.
The rhythmPeaks of euphoria and sharp drops.A steady presence, less spectacular.
Faced with boredomIt collapses as soon as routine sets in.It handles ordinary days perfectly well.

A strong attraction is not a false feeling: it is often the first stage of love. The question is therefore not whether it is « real », but not to make an irreversible decision on the strength of the first three weeks.

What is actually happening in your head

The symptoms you feel are not in your imagination: they match phases that are fairly well described, and they do not kick in at the same time or for the same reasons.

Three mechanisms follow and overlap each other.

Attraction and the reward system

At the start the brain runs on anticipation. Every message, every glance triggers a burst of pleasure, and the wait between two contacts amplifies everything. That explains the early euphoria, the missing appetite and the short nights. This phase is powerful, but it is also the least reliable for judging your feelings.

Attachment, which takes longer

Attachment, by contrast, is built through repetition and presence. It produces calm rather than excitement, which is precisely why it is less noticeable. Many people believe their feelings are fading when those feelings are simply changing in nature. Our attachment style test shows how this mechanism differs from person to person.

Idealisation, which always drops away

At the beginning the brain fills in the blanks: whatever we do not know about the other person, we imagine in their favour. That idealisation lasts from a few weeks to a few months, then dissolves. The moment it drops is the real test: either the feelings collapse with it, or they hold, and it was indeed love.

The test says you are in love. Now what?

Knowing what you feel settles one question and opens another, often more uncomfortable one: what to do with it, especially when you have no idea whether it is mutual.

A few markers, without the magazine advice.

What does not help

✗

Waiting for the perfect moment

It never comes. The perfect moment is an elegant way of postponing a frightening conversation indefinitely, and waiting does not make things easier, it only makes them heavier.

✗

Hunting for signs everywhere

Analysing a reply delay or an emoji will teach you nothing reliable. You will always find what you are looking for, one way or the other. Only a real conversation gives a real answer.

What actually helps

1

Test it against ordinary time

Spend time together in situations that flatter nobody: a wait, an inconvenience, a day with nothing special in it. Attraction gets bored quickly, love does not. It is the most reliable test there is, and it is free.

2

Say it plainly

A solemn declaration intimidates everyone, you first. Saying that this person matters a great deal to you is enough to open the door, without cornering them or exposing yourself more than necessary.

3

Look at what comes back to you

The intensity of what you give says nothing about the relationship. Count instead what comes back: the questions you are asked, the effort made to see you, the attention paid to what you say. A living bond shows in that reciprocity.

4

Accept not knowing right away

Feelings take time to settle, and that is perfectly fine. If your score sits between two bands, take the test again in a month: the gap between the two results will often tell you more than the score itself.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I am in love or just attracted?

The most reliable criterion is not intensity but how well you know the other person. As long as they seem perfect to you, what you love is mainly the picture you have of them. When you know their flaws, some of them irritate you and your feelings hold anyway, it is love. A second clue: attraction collapses under routine, love goes through it undamaged.

What is the difference between loving someone and being in love?

Loving someone means caring about them and wanting their good, which also applies to a friend or a family member. Being in love adds desire, exclusivity and the wish for a particular place in their life. That is why you can love a person deeply without being in love with them, and why « I love you but I am not in love with you » is not a contradiction at all.

How long does the early in-love phase last?

The exhilarating phase, the one with obsessive thoughts and idealisation, generally lasts from a few months to around two years. It does not vanish into nothing: it makes way for a calmer, steadier attachment. Many couples read that shift as a loss of feeling when it is really a change of nature.

Can you be in love with someone you barely know?

You can feel a very strong attraction for someone you hardly know, and it is perfectly real. But love in the full sense requires knowing the person, including what makes them imperfect. That is why love at first sight is a possible starting point and never a conclusion.

Is this « am I in love » test reliable?

It is a thinking tool, not a diagnosis. It rests on markers psychologists associate with romantic attachment, but it is only worth what your answers are worth, and your feelings can change from one month to the next. Taking it again a few weeks later is in fact a good way to see which way things are moving.

The takeaway

Being in love is not measured by the strength of what you feel, but by what remains once the early excitement subsides. If you know this person's flaws and your feelings hold, the question is settled.

This test is a thinking tool, not a verdict. It gives you a vocabulary and an order of magnitude, nothing more. Nobody, this test included, knows your feelings better than you do: it simply helps put words on what you already half knew.
