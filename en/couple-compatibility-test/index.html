What is a couple test and what is it for?

A couple test is an assessment tool designed to measure the compatibility and harmony between two partners. Unlike a casual conversation, it structures your exchanges around key themes: communication, trust, values, life goals, and intimacy.

The goal isn't to deliver a final verdict, but to offer an objective mirror of your relationship. Your score highlights your strengths and identifies the areas where a joint effort could strengthen your bond.

The questions are built on concrete everyday situations, not abstract concepts. That's what makes the exercise relevant and accessible, whether your relationship is new or years old.

The 5 pillars of a strong relationship

Twenty questions cannot cover everything, so they focus on what makes a relationship last. Here are the five pillars being measured.

The milestones passed

Meeting the family, being introduced to friends, going on holiday together, being included in the important moments. These steps are not formalities: each one says the relationship has been owned in front of someone other than the two of you.

The ordeals survived

Grief, illness, job loss, a betrayal, a stretch of distance. A couple is not judged on its good days but on what it does with the bad ones. What has held once will hold more easily the next time.

Everyday anchoring

Living together, sharing a lease, an account or a pet, knowing where the other stands financially, deciding as a pair. Material anchoring is not romantic, but it is what turns a story into a shared life.

A shared projection

Knowing where you are heading, having settled the big decisions, having personal plans that pull the same way. A couple that cannot talk about five years from now often already knows the answer.

Security of the bond

Not fearing that the other will leave, not seeing a break-up brandished in every argument, being able to count on them in an emergency, knowing how to repair after a conflict. This is the pillar that makes all the others possible.

How to interpret your couple test results

Not every question carries the same weight. Being unable to picture five years from now counts for more than one badly handled weekend alone: each question carries a weight matching what it really says about solidity, and your score is what your answers earn against the total of those weights.

The verdict runs on eight levels, from a relationship in pain to an almost perfect match: fine enough that two different couples don't get the same label. You each also get your individual score, and the gap between the two is often the most interesting data point of the test.

The score is just a starting point. What truly matters is what you do with it together.

Mistakes to avoid when taking a couple test

For the result to actually mean something, here are the most common traps:

Not answering honestly

Answering what you think your partner wants to hear completely skews the results. The purpose is to reveal your real thoughts, not to please the other person. Honesty is the key to a meaningful test.

Taking the score too seriously

An online test doesn't replace deeper work on your relationship. Think of it as a conversation starter, not a clinical diagnosis.

Comparing with other couples

Every couple is unique. Comparing your score to your friends' results is pointless. Focus on your own progress as a couple.

💡 Tip: Retake the test in a few months to track your growth. A couple that grows together is a couple that lasts.

When should you take a couple test?

There's no perfect moment, but some situations particularly lend themselves to it:

Early in the relationship: to make sure you're aligned on the important topics before committing further.

After a rough patch: an argument, growing apart, falling into routine… The test helps you take stock without judgment.

Before a big decision: moving in together, getting married, having a child. The touchy subjects, money, family, parenting, don't vanish at the signature: compatibility before marriage is tested precisely on those.

Just out of curiosity: even happy couples benefit from checking in regularly. Think of it as relationship maintenance.

10 signs your relationship is on the right track

Beyond the score, here are the concrete markers of a healthy relationship:

You communicate openly

You can bring up any topic without fear of judgment. Disagreements are settled through conversation, not silence.

You support each other

In good times and in tough ones, you're each other's number one supporter. You make a solid team.

You maintain your individuality

Each of you has your own friends, passions, and alone time. Independence within interdependence is a real strength.

You laugh together

Humour and complicity are powerful glue. Couples who laugh together still argue, but they repair faster: the inside joke that defuses a tension is worth every communication technique.

You have shared goals

You envision a future together, whether it's a weekend getaway, a move, or a life-changing project.

How to improve your couple score

If the result reveals room for improvement, here are concrete leads:

Schedule a weekly check-in

Set aside 30 minutes a week for an emotional check-in. Talk about how you feel, not just the week's logistics. The hardest part is starting: prepare one open question in advance, and let the other person finish their sentences.

Practice active listening

Listening is more than just staying quiet. It means rephrasing, validating your partner's emotions, and asking open-ended questions.

Nurture the small gestures

A sweet message, a coffee made just right, a spontaneous compliment, micro-gestures have more impact than you might think.

Don't shy away from tough topics

Money, intimacy, family, the future, the subjects we avoid are often the ones that create the most distance when left unspoken.

One question often leads to another

Twenty questions give you the big picture. Depending on what the result stirred up, here is where to dig, because a deep doubt, a curiosity and a need for lightness don't call for the same tool:

The test woke up something uncomfortable

If what surfaced goes beyond everyday disagreement, exhaustion, fear of doing wrong, the feeling of walking on eggshells, the question is no longer compatibility but the health of the relationship. The signs of a toxic relationship aren't measured with the same questions.

You want to check how well you know each other

A good compatibility score doesn't say whether you know the other person's details. Getting their favourite dish wrong never killed a relationship, but guessing their answers live, the way who knows their partner best works, teaches things no score can show.

Miles keep you apart

Distance changes which questions matter: trust, communication and missing each other look different when you don't share a daily life. A long-distance relationship deserves its own criteria.

You just want something light

Not everything has to be a diagnosis. An evening of spicy questions or absurd debates sometimes does more for complicity than one big serious conversation, and that too is how you look after a relationship.

What research says about happy couples

Relationship psychology research shows that thriving couples share certain habits. The renowned researcher John Gottman found that stable couples maintain a ratio of 5 positive interactions for every 1 negative one.

Another key finding: it's not the absence of conflict that makes a great couple, but how they handle it. Couples who treat each other with respect, even during an argument, report significantly higher satisfaction levels.

The questions on this page draw on that research by looking at concrete areas of your life together. Each one is designed to reveal not “who is right”, but how you work as a pair.

A happy couple isn't a couple without problems. It's a couple that knows how to face them together.

Frequently asked questions about the couple test

Is the couple test really free?
Yes, our couple test is 100% free with no signup required. You can retake it as many times as you like to track how your relationship evolves.
How long does the couple test take?
The couple test takes about 5 minutes. It has 20 questions, which you can work through together in SOLO mode or each in turn in DUO mode.
How is the couple test score calculated?
Each question carries a weight matching how much it says about a couple's solidity, and each answer a value matching what it reveals. The final score is what your answers earn against the total available, shown as a percentage and then placed on one of eight bands.
My score is low, should I be worried?
Not necessarily! A low score simply means you have different perspectives on certain topics. It's an invitation to talk, not a verdict. The strongest couples are those who know how to discuss their differences.
Can we retake the couple test multiple times?
Absolutely! We actually encourage you to retake the test regularly (every 3 to 6 months) to see how your relationship evolves. The 20 questions are drawn from a pool of 30, so a retake won't give you the same series.
