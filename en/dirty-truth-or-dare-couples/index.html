One deck that runs from suggestive to very direct

A dirty truth or dare that starts too hard never works. The first dare makes someone uncomfortable, one of you shuts down, and the evening is over before it began. That's why this deck builds up gradually instead of going in hard from the first card.

The gentlest cards stay suggestive: questions that make you blush, dares that bring you closer, nothing that forces you to expose yourself. The most direct ones drop the padding entirely and assume you both know where this is going. The draw mixes them together, and the right to skip is what keeps you in control.

Start gently, even if you reckon you can skip that stage. The laughter of those first cards does more for the rest of the evening than any dare thrown in cold…

How it is played

The same as the classic version, plus one extra point that matters a lot more here.

1

Enter both first names

The game announces whose turn it is on every card, so you never have to work out who's playing.

2

Truth or dare, you decide

The named player calls their side, and the card comes out at random from the matching deck. You never see the next one.

3

Passing is built into the game

Refusing a card lands a forfeit, and that's it. It's the most important rule of this version: it makes skipping unremarkable instead of turning it into a confession.

4

You set the limit, not the game

Decide before you start what's off the table tonight. A game never replaces an agreement between the two of you, it only makes suggestions.

Consent, put plainly

This game suggests, it imposes nothing. Every card can be refused without explanation, as often as needed, and a forfeit is never a punishment you have to endure. If one of you isn't in the mood, the right answer is to stop the game. Not to negotiate.

The cards are written for a couple playing together, between consenting adults. They assume you know each other, and that you'll both know when to stop.

Frequently asked questions

How is it different from the classic version?

The cards, quite simply. The classic version is for every couple and can be played in front of anyone. This one has its own deck, noticeably more direct, written for a moment when it's just the two of you.

Is it very explicit?

It runs from suggestive to very direct. The gentlest ask for a compliment or a kiss, the boldest assume an intimacy that's already there. There's no level to pick: the right to skip is what sets the limit, card after card.

How many cards are there?

70 in total: 30 truths, 24 dares and 16 forfeits in a single deck. They don't come back around until the deck runs dry, so there's enough for a whole evening without repeats.

Is it suitable for a couple who have just met?

The start of the deck, absolutely! It works beautifully for testing the water. The most direct cards assume you already know each other well: if one lands too early, skip it, that's what it's there for.

Do I need an account?

No, and nothing is stored. The game runs in your browser, your answers never leave the device, and there's no game history at all.

What a game like this can genuinely do

It isn't going to transform your intimate life, and nobody should ask it to. What it does well is give a frame to a conversation many couples have never really had, the same one the spicy quiz approaches through questions rather than dares. Because it's the card asking the question, not you.

The rest is yours to decide. And that's exactly as it should be!
